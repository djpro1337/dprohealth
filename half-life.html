01What is half-life?

The time it takes for half of a compound to leave your body. After one half-life 50% is left, after two 25%, and after five only about 3%.

02Why it matters

Dose faster than a compound clears and it builds up. Dose slower and levels fall to almost nothing between doses. Half-life explains why some compounds are dosed daily and others weekly.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly is a half-life?

The time it takes for the amount of a compound in your body to fall by half. It follows a fixed pattern: 50% remains after one half-life, 25% after two, 12.5% after three, and about 3% after five. That's why "5 half-lives" is the usual rule of thumb for "mostly gone."

Why does half-life matter for dosing frequency?

It decides how much is still around when the next dose lands. If the interval is long compared with the half-life, each dose starts from nearly zero and levels swing from high to low. If the interval is short compared with the half-life, doses overlap and levels climb until they plateau.

What is build-up (accumulation)?

When you dose again before the last dose has cleared, the leftovers add up. Levels keep rising with each dose until the amount clearing between doses equals one dose. The build-up factor tells you how much higher that plateau is than a single dose. Weekly semaglutide, for example, builds to roughly double a single dose.

What is steady state, and why does it take so long for weekly compounds?

Steady state takes about 5 half-lives no matter how often you dose. A compound with a 2-hour half-life gets there in about 10 hours. One with a 7-day half-life takes about 5 weeks. That's one reason weekly GLP-1s are increased slowly: each dose change takes weeks to fully show up.

What is washout?

How long it takes to clear a compound after your last dose: about 5 half-lives to drop roughly 97%. For BPC-157 that's a few hours. For semaglutide or testosterone cypionate, it's over a month.

How do esters work (enanthate, cypionate, decanoate…)?

An ester is a chain attached to the hormone that makes it release slowly from an oil injection. For injectables, the half-life listed is how long the depot takes to release and clear, not the hormone itself (free testosterone clears in minutes). Longer esters mean fewer injections and steadier levels, but they also take longer to settle and longer to wash out.

Why are some numbers marked "Estimate" or "Animal data only"?

Many research peptides have never had a proper human pharmacokinetic study. When the only data is from rats or dogs, or there's no published data at all, we say so instead of presenting a number as fact. Use the Override switch if you have a better figure.
